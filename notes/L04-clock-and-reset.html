슬라이드 5쪽이 한 줄을 더한다. "Delay between flip-flops caused by logic gates, muxes, wires". flip-flop 두 개 사이에 놓인 조합 논리와 배선이 만드는 지연이고, 기호는 Tcomb다. 설계자가 직접 줄일 수 있는 유일한 항목이라는 점에서 나머지 셋과 다르다. setup·hold·clock-to-Q는 셀이 정하지만, 이건 내가 쓴 RTL이 정한다.

Slide 5 adds one line: "Delay between flip-flops caused by logic gates, muxes, wires". It is the delay of the combinational logic and wiring sitting between two flops, written Tcomb. It differs from the other three in one important way: it is the only term you can shrink yourself. Setup, hold and clock-to-Q are set by the cell; this one is set by the RTL you wrote.

Lecture 2, §05 said there are bugs from being too slow and bugs from being too fast. Today those two got their names: setup and hold.

And this is exactly why hold violations are the frightening ones. Dropping the clock on the tester does not help, so once silicon exists there is essentially no fix. It is the one timing bug you cannot ship your way out of by derating the frequency.

현장 감각On the job

STA 리포트에 경로 하나가 slack = -0.12 ns로 찍혔다. 팀에서 두 가지 제안이 나왔다. (a) 동작 주파수를 1.2 GHz에서 1.1 GHz로 낮춘다. (b) 그 경로의 조합 논리를 두 단으로 쪼개서 파이프라인을 하나 더 넣는다. 둘 다 되는가? 그리고 무엇을 먼저 확인해야 하는가?

An STA report shows one path at slack = -0.12 ns. Two proposals come up in the team: (a) drop the target from 1.2 GHz to 1.1 GHz, or (b) split the combinational logic and add a pipeline stage. Do both work, and what do you check first?

생각해본 뒤 펼치기Think first, then open

먼저 확인할 것은 그게 setup slack인지 hold slack인지다. 슬라이드 7쪽의 Tslack 정의는 setup 쪽 식이고, "slack"이라고만 쓰면 보통 setup을 가리키지만 리포트에는 둘 다 나온다.

  • setup slack이면 (a)와 (b) 둘 다 통한다. 주기를 늘리면 좌변이 커지고, 경로를 쪼개면 Tcomb가 줄어든다.
  • hold slack이면 (a)는 아무 효과가 없다. hold 부등식에 T가 아예 없기 때문이다. (b)도 방향이 반대다. 파이프라인을 넣는 건 경로를 짧게 만드는 일인데, hold는 경로가 너무 짧아서 나는 문제다. 지연을 넣어야 한다.

현장에서 이 구별을 놓치면 며칠을 날린다. 주파수를 내려서 재실행했는데 같은 경로가 그대로 빨간색이면, 그건 도구가 이상한 게 아니라 hold였던 것이다.

First establish whether that is setup slack or hold slack. The Tslack definition on slide 7 is the setup formula, and an unqualified "slack" usually means setup, but a real report carries both.

  • If it is setup slack, both (a) and (b) work. A longer period grows the left side; splitting the path shrinks Tcomb.
  • If it is hold slack, (a) does nothing at all, because T never appears in the hold inequality. And (b) pushes the wrong way: pipelining shortens the path, and hold is the problem of the path being too short. You need to add delay.

Missing this distinction costs days. If you lower the frequency, re-run, and the same path is still red, the tool is not broken: it was a hold path.

Where clocks come from

RTL을 쓸 때 clk는 그냥 포트 하나였다. 그 포트에 무엇이 연결되는지가 여기서 처음 나온다.

While writing RTL, clk was just a port. This is the first time the lecture says what is actually on the other end of it.

슬라이드가 적은 클럭의 정의The slide's definition of a clock

"A clock is a periodic signal oscillating between a high and a low state at a constant frequency. Mainly used for synchronization and coordination of operations and data movement." (슬라이드가 출처를 Wikipedia로 적어놨다)

"A clock is a periodic signal oscillating between a high and a low state at a constant frequency. Mainly used for synchronization and coordination of operations and data movement." The slide credits Wikipedia for this one.

읽고 넘기기 쉬운 문장인데 뒷문장이 이 강의 전체의 전제다. 클럭은 "빠르게 하려고" 있는 게 아니라 여러 회로가 같은 박자에 움직이게 하려고 있다. 클럭이 두 개가 되는 순간 그 합의가 깨지는데, 05절부터가 그 이야기다.

Easy to skim past, but the second sentence is the premise of the whole lecture. A clock is not there to make things fast; it is there to make separate circuits move on the same beat. The moment there are two clocks, that agreement breaks, and that is what §05 onwards is about.

  • 가짜 엣지의 폭이 너무 좁으면 flip-flop이 완전히 잡지도, 완전히 무시하지도 못한다. 06절의 metastability와 같은 자리로 간다

그래서 "AND 게이트 하나면 되는 거 아닌가"가 위험한 생각이다. 맞는 말인데 맞는 세계가 다르다. 논리적으로는 맞고 물리적으로는 틀렸다.

A glitch on a data signal is harmless as long as it settles before the next clock edge, which is why glitches are easy to dismiss. A glitch on a clock signal is a different animal.

  • every flop on that clock runs one cycle that never existed. Counters count once more, state machines advance once more
  • if the fake edge is narrow enough, the flop neither fully captures nor fully ignores it, which lands you in the metastability of §06
  • simulation often misses it. Without accurate gate delays, the glitch simply does not exist in RTL simulation

Which is why "surely one AND gate is enough" is a dangerous thought. It is correct, just in the wrong world: logically right, physically wrong.

  • The interesting part is that 1 and 2 give the same answer. The test is not "same frequency" but "can you compute the gap between the edges?" A divided-by-two clock has a different frequency, yet every second edge lines up with the original, so the relationship is fixed and STA can be told about it.
  • Pair 3 is not an integer ratio. 100 to 80 is 4:5, and with two independent PLLs the phase keeps sliding over time. Some cycles the edges coincide and some they do not. "Sometimes they coincide" is exactly the CDC hazard.

So "different frequency means async" fails on pair 2. The real test is whether they share a source, and therefore whether you can write the relationship down.

각 비트가 독립적으로 동기화되기 때문이다. 비트 하나하나는 옛값 아니면 새값으로 정착하는데, 어느 비트가 어느 쪽으로 갈지는 따로 정해진다. 그래서 받는 쪽에 나타나는 값이 옛값도 새값도 아닌 것이 될 수 있다.

Because each bit is synchronised independently. Every single bit settles to either the old value or the new one, but each makes that choice on its own. The value that appears on the receiving side can therefore be neither the old value nor the new one.

"Design mistakes that allow metastability or inconsistent sampling to cause functional failures"

"Design mistakes that allow metastability or inconsistent sampling to cause functional failures"

이 한 줄에 06절의 두 문제가 그대로 들어 있다. metastability는 flop 하나가 불확정에 빠지는 것이고 inconsistent sampling은 멀티비트 버스의 data coherency 문제다. 그리고 "design mistakes"라고 못 박은 게 중요하다. 이건 공정 편차나 노이즈 문제가 아니라 RTL을 그렇게 써서 생긴 것이므로 정적 분석으로 잡을 수 있다.

Both problems from §06 are in that one line. Metastability is one flop going undecided; inconsistent sampling is the data coherency problem on a multi-bit bus. And the phrase "design mistakes" is doing real work: this is not process variation or noise, it is something the RTL did, which is why static analysis can find it.

여기서 얻을 실무 감각은 이거다. CDC 도구를 돌리는 일의 절반은 constraint를 쓰는 일이다. "버튼 누르고 기다리면 답이 나온다"가 아니다.

The full day is the instructor's own experience. The speaker notes say: "I have had experience where the runs take as long as a day to complete, where we start the run and wait for a day for the results to pop up". The slide body only says "significant run times".

Why it is slow. The slide gives the reason: "As the design complexity increases, the number of combinations grows exponentially". The tool examines every data and control path combination across every pair of clock domains. Add blocks and the combinations multiply.

What to change: constraints. The slide says that too: "The tools often require manual constraints to manage noise and false violations. Static, constant, etc."

  • tell the tool that a signal is actually constant, like a configuration register written once at boot, and it stops looking at that path entirely
  • the same goes for paths you can declare static
  • constraints come from the SDC file or are written by hand (speaker notes)

The nice part is that one action buys two things: the same constraints that cut false violations also cut run time. In the instructor's words, "we are basically helping the tool reduce its burden which in turn helps us with speedup".

The practical takeaway: half the work of running a CDC tool is writing its constraints. It is not a button you press and wait for an answer.

Sync reset과 Async reset

Sync and async reset

여기서부터 강의 후반부다. 클럭 대신 reset이 도메인을 넘는다.

The last stretch of the lecture. Now it is reset, not data, that crosses domains.

Lecture 3와의 연결. Lecture 3의 18절이 두 코드가 다른 회로라는 것과 구별 기준(reset이 sensitivity list에 있는가)까지 이미 줬다. 그래서 여기서는 문법을 다시 설명하지 않는다. 이 강의가 더하는 것은 "그래서 어느 쪽을 고르는가"와 "왜 그런가"다.

Where this joins Lecture 3. Lecture 3, §18 already gave you that these are two different circuits and the test that separates them: is the reset in the sensitivity list? So the syntax is not repeated here. What this lecture adds is which one to pick, and why.

이게 결정적이다. 02절에서 PLL이 lock될 때까지 클럭이 안 나온다고 했다. 전원이 막 들어온 칩에는 아직 클럭이 없다. 그 상태에서 동기 reset은 아무것도 못 한다. 초기화되지 않은 flip-flop들이 임의의 값을 들고 임의의 조합 논리를 구동하고 있는데, 그걸 멈출 방법이 없는 것이다.

그래서 업계의 답은 "둘 중 하나"가 아니라 절충이다. 발표자 노트가 그 절충을 한 줄로 말한다. "Once the clocks become stable, the reset can be safely deasserted, typically in a synchronized manner."

  • assert(리셋을 건다)는 async로. 클럭이 없어도 즉시 들어야 하니까
  • de-assert(리셋을 푼다)는 sync로. 이때는 클럭이 돌고 있고, 푸는 순간이 클럭 엣지와 겹치면 사고가 나니까

이 두 줄이 10절과 11절 전체의 요약이다. 그리고 Lecture 3에는 없던 내용이다. 거기서는 "둘은 다른 회로다"까지였다.

Read the table alone and async wins every row, which feels suspicious. The real reason is in the speaker notes.

"During power-up, the PLLs and clock tree may not yet be operating. A synchronous reset cannot reset the flip-flops until a clock edge arrives. An asynchronous reset, however, directly drives the reset input of each flip-flop, allowing the entire chip to be initialized immediately."

That is the deciding argument. §02 said no clock comes out until the PLL locks. A chip that just got power has no clock yet, and a synchronous reset can do nothing in that state. Uninitialised flops are holding arbitrary values and driving arbitrary logic, with no way to stop them.

So the industry answer is not "one or the other" but a split. The speaker notes state it in a line: "Once the clocks become stable, the reset can be safely deasserted, typically in a synchronized manner."

  • assert asynchronously, because it has to take effect with no clock available
  • de-assert synchronously, because by then the clock is running and releasing on top of an edge causes damage

Those two lines are the summary of §10 and §11. And they are new relative to Lecture 3, which stopped at "they are different circuits".

규칙 한 줄The rule, in one line

슬라이드가 bullet 두 개로 적은 것을 합치면 이렇다.

rst2가 걸릴 때는 rst1이 걸렸든 풀렸든 상관없다. 그러나 rst1이 걸릴 때는 rst2도 반드시 걸려 있어야 한다.

이 조건은 비대칭이다. "두 reset을 동시에 건다"가 아니라 "한쪽이 다른 쪽을 포함한다"에 가깝다. 09절의 전파 깊이 서열(Cold POR > Warm POR > HW > SW)이 여기에 그대로 대응한다. 깊은 reset이 걸리면 얕은 reset이 통제하는 영역도 당연히 reset 상태여야 한다.

슬라이드가 말하지 않는 것: 이 순서를 회로로 어떻게 강제하는지는 안 나온다. 슬라이드는 "이런 조건을 갖도록 하라(make sure we have some reset conditions)"까지만 말한다. 그러니 시험 대비로는 "무엇을 지켜야 하는가"까지만 외우면 되고, 구현은 이 강의 밖이다.

Merging the slide's two bullets gives:

When rst2 asserts, it does not matter whether rst1 is asserted or not. But when rst1 asserts, rst2 must be asserted as well.

The condition is asymmetric. It is not "assert both together" so much as "one contains the other". The propagation depth ordering from §09 (cold POR, warm POR, HW, SW) maps onto it directly: when a deeper reset fires, the territory of a shallower one had better be in reset too.

What the slide does not say: how you enforce that ordering in a circuit. It goes as far as "make sure we have some reset conditions" and stops. For exam purposes, learn what has to hold; the implementation is outside this lecture.

그래서 여기서는 억지로 짝을 만들지 않았다. 이 절을 읽고 "회사별 용어 표가 왜 없지"를 다시 찾아 헤매지 않도록 적어둔다. CDC, RDC, metastability, recovery time은 Accellera 표준 문서에까지 같은 이름으로 올라간 용어이므로 회사가 갈릴 여지도 적다.

다만 약어 표기는 갈린다. 슬라이드가 풀네임으로 쓴 Integrated Gated Clock Cell을 업계 문서와 합성 리포트에서는 대개 ICG로 줄여 쓴다. 슬라이드에 약어가 안 나온다는 것을 알고 있으면 된다.

Lecture 1 had a table of Intel, NXP and NVIDIA calling the same activity different things. This lecture has nothing like it. Even with a Marvell engineer presenting, neither the slides nor the speaker notes mention a single company-specific term.

So no pairing was invented here. This paragraph exists so that nobody goes hunting for the missing table. CDC, RDC, metastability and recovery time are names that made it into an Accellera standard, which leaves little room for company dialects.

What does vary is abbreviation. The slide spells out Integrated Gated Clock Cell; industry documents and synthesis reports usually shorten it to ICG. It is enough to know the slide never abbreviates it.

Lecture 5, FIFO design (Wednesday 9 September, guest lecturer Igor Miranda). The FIFO synchroniser that got one card in §07 today takes a whole lecture. Gray-coded pointers, full and empty generation and depth sizing all live there. And Lab 1 follows right after.

What carries straight over is the double-FF synchroniser, gray code, and the instinct that values arrive late across an asynchronous boundary. That last one is why FIFO full and empty have to be generated conservatively: a pointer that crossed is always a few cycles stale.
